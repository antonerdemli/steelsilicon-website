Steel & Silicon

The Nation Simulator

Steel & Silicon: The Nation Simulator is a planned nation-management simulation. Take charge of a country in 1946 and guide it through more than seven decades of industrial, social, and technological change.

Explore the mechanics ↓Follow the project ↗
01An era of change
1946—2020

From post-war industry to the digital state.

From post-war reconstruction to the digital age, the state will move from mechanization and mass production to automation and global communications. Oil, gas, and nuclear power will drive industrial growth, while motorization, aviation, and container shipping reshape the world's economic geography.

It will have to build faster, produce more, advance science, modernize infrastructure, restructure the economy, and respond to society's rising expectations. The space race, nuclear industry, computerization, and digital revolution will become not just symbols of their age, but results of the state's ability to turn scientific advances into tangible national development.

04Core mechanics

Change the course of history
as any of 120 modern nations

The economy, industry, population, science, infrastructure, and politics form one interconnected system. Progress in one area inevitably changes the others, and every decision has long-term consequences.

01

Economy and markets

A unified economic system connects businesses, goods, markets, employment, and household consumption.

  • Production chains. Businesses purchase raw materials, hire workers, and produce goods.
  • Provincial markets. Prices depend on supply, demand, inventories, and delivery costs.
  • Living economy. Private firms independently open, expand, and close production.
  • Trade and shortages. Goods move between provinces and countries, while shortages raise prices and reduce living standards.
02

Industry and resources

Extraction, processing, and manufacturing form a single system. The game features 60 goods.

  • Production chains. Raw materials become equipment, vehicles, electronics, food, and other goods.
  • Industrial location. Efficiency depends on resources, energy, workers, and transport.
  • Technological development. Mechanization and automation raise productivity and unlock new industries.
  • Industrial dependence. A shortage of one resource can halt an entire chain and force the state to rely on imports.
03

Population and society

People work, consume goods, move, and place growing demands on their living conditions.

  • Demographics. Birth rates, mortality, life expectancy, and migration determine the size of the population.
  • Living standards. Income, prices, housing, education, and healthcare shape people's prosperity.
  • Labor and consumption. The population supplies workers to the economy and creates demand for goods and services.
  • Migration and urbanization. People move in search of work, cities grow, and regional inequality fuels discontent.
04

Government and politics

The political system defines the structure of the state, how power is formed, and the scope for reform.

  • Form of government. The state structure determines how authority is distributed and leadership changes hands.
  • Elections. Results depend on living standards, public sentiment, and trust in government.
  • Support and reform. Successful policies strengthen stability, while change requires time and strong institutions.
  • Internal crises. Conflicts of interest can lead to protests, mass unrest, and struggles for power.
05

Public finance and administration

Taxes, the budget, public debt, and government administration form one financial system.

  • Taxes. Tax policy affects the income of the budget, businesses, and households.
  • The budget. Funds are allocated to infrastructure, education, healthcare, science, social policy, and public administration.
  • Deficits and debt. Spending beyond revenue can overcome a crisis but increases future obligations.
  • Administration and resilience. Public institutions collect taxes and provide services, while reserves fund critical imports.
06

Scientific advances and technology

Science raises productivity, unlocks new technologies, and strengthens the state's independence.

  • Research institutes. Eight institutes advance construction, industry, agriculture, energy, computing, economics, medicine, and chemistry.
  • Scientific development. Each institute progresses through five levels and gradually unlocks more complex research.
  • Practical application. Technology speeds construction, raises productivity, and reduces resource consumption.
  • Licenses. Domestic innovations can be sold, while foreign licenses help close a technological gap.
07

Infrastructure

Infrastructure connects regions and carries goods, energy, and information.

  • Transport network. Roads, railways, ports, and airports support transport and foreign trade.
  • Energy. Power stations and transmission lines supply businesses, cities, and public institutions.
  • Oil and gas. Main pipelines move fuel, while gas storage facilities maintain essential reserves.
  • Telecommunications. Telephone, satellite, and digital networks accelerate information exchange and state operations.
08

Construction and land development

The state's limited territory is gradually developed for housing, industry, and infrastructure.

  • Land fund. Districts are divided into land categories for different forms of construction and economic activity.
  • Site development. Undeveloped land must be prepared and connected to infrastructure.
  • Land and property. The state sells plots, while private construction expands residential, commercial, and industrial property.
  • Construction. Every project requires time, workers, machinery, and building materials.
